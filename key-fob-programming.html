Fob Types, Explained

  • Remote fob. Separate remote that locks, unlocks, and opens the trunk.
  • Remote head key. Key blade and remote buttons in one piece.
  • Flip key. The blade folds into the remote and pops out with a button.
  • Proximity or smart key. Stays in your pocket or bag. The car senses it, unlocks the doors, and allows push-button starting.
  • Push-to-start key. A smart key the car must recognize before the engine starts.

Key Fob Not Working After a Battery Change?

Don't assume it needs reprogramming. Common causes:

  • The battery is upside down, the wrong type, or not touching the contacts
  • A button or circuit board got damaged when the case was opened
  • The fob lost its sync with the car, which some vehicles need to reset after a battery swap

We'll check the battery, fob, and your car's response before programming or replacing anything.

Can I Program a Key Fob Myself?

Sometimes. Some older vehicles let owners add a remote with a sequence of key turns and button presses found in the owner's manual. Most newer cars, especially those with smart keys or push-to-start, need professional diagnostic equipment. If you try and the fob still won't pair, call us.

Can You Program a Fob I Bought Online?

Often, yes. But online fobs must match your vehicle's frequency, electronics, and part number exactly. Many cheap listings don't. Send us the fob's brand and part number along with your vehicle details, and we'll confirm whether it will work before you book.

How Much Does Key Fob Programming Cost?

OEM fobs cost more than aftermarket ones. Key cutting, if your fob includes a blade, adds a little. Ask for the total price before we begin.
