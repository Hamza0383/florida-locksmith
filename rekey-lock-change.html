What Is Lock Rekeying?

Every lock has a set of small pins inside the cylinder. The pins line up only when the right key is inserted. When we rekey a lock, we swap those pins for a new set that matches a new key.

After a rekey:

  • Your old keys no longer turn the lock.
  • New keys work perfectly.
  • Your deadbolts, knobs, and handles stay in place.
  • Several locks can be set to work with the same key.
  • Businesses can set up a master key system.

That's the main difference from a lock change. Rekeying changes what's inside the cylinder. Replacing swaps out the whole lock.

How We Rekey Your Locks

1

Identify the lock

We check the brand, cylinder type, keyway, and condition to make sure the lock can be rekeyed and is worth keeping.

2

Remove the cylinder

We take out the part of the lock that holds the pins.

3

Swap the pins

We remove the old pins and set new ones matched to your new key.

4

Test the new key

We turn it in the lock several times to make sure it operates smoothly.

5

Make sure the old key fails

If you have an old key handy, we try it to confirm it no longer works.

6

Match other locks if you want

We set every lock you choose to the same key, so one key opens your front door, back door, and garage.

Why Rekey Instead of Replace?

Old keys stop working

Whoever has a copy of your old key, whether a previous owner, ex, tenant, or former employee, can't use it anymore.

You keep hardware you already like

If your locks work well and look fine, there's no reason to throw them out. Rekeying keeps them in service.

Usually costs less

Since you're paying to change pins rather than buy new hardware, rekeying typically runs cheaper than a full lock change. The final price depends on how many locks you have, the lock type, and whether you want them keyed alike.

One key for the whole house

While we're rekeying, we can set all your compatible locks to one key.

When Should You Rekey Your Locks?

🏡

After moving into a new home

You have no idea how many keys to your new home are out there. Previous owners, renters, contractors, cleaners, and real estate agents may all have had copies. Rekeying right after closing puts you back in control.

🔑

After losing your keys

If you can't find your keys, someone else might. Rekeying makes the lost set useless, which matters most if your keys were with anything showing your address.

🚫

After a divorce or breakup

When someone moves out, rekeying is a quick way to make sure their key no longer works, without replacing hardware.

📅

Between tenants

Landlords rekey between renters so former tenants can't get back in. It's faster and cheaper than replacing every lock on every turnover.

🏢

After employee changes

Businesses rekey when staff leave or roles change. A master key system lets managers open everything while each employee only opens what they need.

🛡

After any security concern

If you think a key was copied without permission or something just doesn't feel right, rekeying resets who has access.

Can Every Lock Brand Be Rekeyed?

Most common brands can, including Schlage, Kwikset, Yale, Weiser, and many commercial cylinders. High-security locks like Mul-T-Lock can often be rekeyed too, but they may need special parts or authorization. Brands use different keyways, which matters if you want one key for everything: a Schlage key won't fit a Kwikset lock. We'll check each lock and tell you what's possible.

How Much Does Rekeying Cost in Jacksonville?

Price depends on how many cylinders you have, the brand and type of lock, whether you want them keyed alike or on a master key system, and whether it's an after-hours call. Rekeying one lock costs less than rekeying a whole house, though the per-lock price often drops when you do several at once. Ask for a per-cylinder price or a total quote before approving the work.
