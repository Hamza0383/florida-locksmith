How a Lock Repair Visit Works

1

We look at the whole door

The lock, key, cylinder, latch, strike plate, handle, and how the door sits in the frame. Many "broken locks" are really doors that have shifted.

2

We test everything

We try the key, latch, deadbolt, and handle to see exactly where it sticks or fails.

3

We find the cause

Common culprits include worn parts, a misaligned strike plate, a sagging door, rust, dirt, a worn key, or loose screws.

4

We repair what we can

Often that means adjusting the strike, tightening hardware, cleaning and lubricating the cylinder, or replacing one worn part.

5

We replace what we can't

If the cylinder, latch, or deadbolt is too damaged, we install compatible new hardware, measuring your door first so it fits.

6

We test again

The key should turn smoothly, the latch should catch on its own, and the deadbolt should slide fully into the frame.

Common Lock Problems and What Causes Them

  • Key won't turn. Usually a worn key, worn pins in the cylinder, debris inside, or a door pulling against the bolt. Don't force it, since a snapped key creates a second problem.
  • Lock is stuck. Rust, worn internal parts, or pressure on the latch from a shifted door are the usual causes.
  • Latch won't catch. The latch may be broken, or the strike plate has moved out of line. Either can usually be fixed in one visit.
  • Handle is loose. Mounting screws work loose over time, or internal parts wear out. We tighten, repair, or replace.
  • Lock is corroded. Light surface rust can often be cleaned and treated. If rust has reached the cylinder or latch, replacement is the safer bet.

Understanding Lock Grades

The ANSI/BHMA standard rates many locks in three grades:

  • Grade 1: The highest rating, built for heavy use and the strongest security.
  • Grade 2: Solid residential and light commercial hardware.
  • Grade 3: Basic residential hardware.

A good grade helps, but installation matters just as much. A Grade 1 deadbolt still needs a solid door, a properly installed strike plate, and long screws to do its job.

How Long Do Locks Last Near the Beach?

It depends on the hardware, how exposed the door is, and how well it's maintained. Salt air and humidity speed up corrosion on exterior locks. Watch for rust, discoloration, sticking keys, loose parts, corroded screws, or latches that stop catching. Stainless steel and other corrosion-resistant hardware lasts noticeably longer on the coast. If a lock keeps acting up, have it looked at before it fails completely.
