How Car Key Replacement Works

1

Tell us about your car

Year, make, model, where it's parked, and your VIN if you have it. This tells us the right key blank and programming steps.

2

Prove it's yours

We'll ask for your driver's license plus registration or title. This protects you from anyone else getting a key to your car.

3

We identify the key type

Metal key, transponder, laser-cut, remote, or smart key. Each one needs a different process.

4

We cut the key

Our mobile key machine cuts the blade to your vehicle's exact specs, including laser-cut and high-security profiles.

5

We program it

If your car has a chip, remote, or smart key, we program the new key to your car's immobilizer and locks.

6

We test it

We check the doors, ignition or push-start, and remote buttons before we leave.

Car Key Types, Explained

Key type What it is What replacement involves
Standard metal key Plain key with no chip, mostly older vehicles Cutting only
Transponder key Has a chip the car must recognize to start Cutting and chip programming
Laser-cut key Thicker blade with a groove cut into the face Special cutting machine, usually programming too
Remote head key or fob Key with lock and unlock buttons Cutting plus remote programming
Smart key Push-to-start key that stays in your pocket Correct smart key hardware and vehicle programming

Cutting alone won't start a car that needs a programmed chip. That's why we handle both.

How Much Does Car Key Replacement Cost?

Price depends on your vehicle, the key type, programming needs, OEM versus aftermarket, and your location. A basic metal key costs the least. Transponder keys add programming. Laser-cut keys need special cutting equipment. Smart keys cost the most because of the hardware and programming involved. Ask us for the total price, including cutting, programming, and the trip, before we start.

Locksmith vs. Dealer

For many vehicles, a locksmith costs less than a dealership, and we come to you. Dealers typically need the car brought in, which can mean a tow if you've lost every key, and they may need to order the key. Prices vary by vehicle on both sides, so compare total cost and timing for the same key type.

Lost All Your Car Keys? Here's What to Do

  1. Don't panic. You don't need the old key for us to make a new one on most vehicles.
  2. Find your ID and registration or title.
  3. Note your car's year, make, model, and VIN. The VIN is on your registration, insurance card, or the driver's side of the dashboard, visible through the windshield.
  4. Call us with your location.

If your keys were stolen, tell us. For many vehicles, we can program the car so the old keys no longer start it.

Make a Spare Before You Need One

If you're down to one key, now is the time to get a spare. Copying a working key is faster and costs much less than replacing every key after it's lost.
